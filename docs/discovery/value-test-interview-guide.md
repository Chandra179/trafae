# Value Test — Interview Guide & Recruitment

Companion to `2026-10-opportunity-frame.md` (§3 test plan). Method: problem
interviews about **past behavior** — we are testing the value risk, not
pitching.

---

## 1. Recruitment (5–8 participants, not friends/family)

**Segment:** deliberate self-learners / serious non-fiction readers — people
who regularly look for books to go deep on a topic.

**Where to post** (pick 2–3, follow each community's self-promo rules — offer
to share results back):

- r/SuggestMeABook, r/nonfictionbooks, r/booksuggestions "meta" threads
- Self-learning communities (r/AskAcademia-adjacent, study Discord servers)
- Local book clubs / library reading groups
- Open-access / public-domain enthusiast spaces (Gutenberg forums, r/OpenAccessResearch)

**Recruitment blurb (copy/paste):**

> I'm building a free, open-source book search that finds books you can read
> immediately — across Project Gutenberg, Open Library, Internet Archive, and
> other open catalogs. Before building more, I want to understand how people
> actually hunt for books today. Looking for 15 minutes of your time this
> week: I'll ask how you found your last few books — no selling, no signup.
> First 6 respondents get early access to the prototype.

**Screener question (one line in the DM/reply):** "Roughly how often do you
search for a new book on a specific topic, and where do you usually look
first?" — include only frequent searchers (weekly-ish or more); note their
answer, it's your first frequency data point.

---

## 2. Interview structure (15–20 min, video or in person)

Rules: ask about the **past**, not the future. **Do not pitch** Lux until the
very end (step 5) — and even then only as "want to see what I'm playing
with?". Listen for **frequency, pain, and current alternatives**. Record with
permission; take verbatim quotes.

### Warm-up (2 min)
1. "Tell me a bit about what you've been reading or studying lately."

### Last-time deep dive (8 min) — the core
2. "Think of the last time you went looking for a book on a specific topic.
   Walk me through exactly what you did, from the moment you decided you
   wanted a book."
3. "What did you try first? Then what?"
4. "Where did that land you? Did you end up reading something? Why / why not?"
5. "What was the most annoying part of that?"
6. "How long did the whole hunt take, door to reading?"

### Frequency & workarounds (4 min)
7. "How often does this happen — weekly, monthly?"
8. "Have you built any habits or shortcuts for it? (saved searches, specific
   sites, asking people)"
9. "Has the free / can-legally-read-it-now part ever mattered? Tell me about
   a time it did — or didn't."

### Signals to write down per participant
- Frequency (searches per month) · Pain moments (verbatim) · Sites/tools used,
  in order · Whether "accessible for free now" came up unprompted · Would they
  do anything differently if search were one-stop?

### Only at the end (3 min)
10. "Want to see the thing I'm building?" → let them run 2 topics of their
    choice through the prototype. Watch in silence: do they reach a book they
    want to start? Do they use the genre chips or the provider strip? Do they
    trust the "why these results" info?

---

## 3. Scoring against the pass/fail criteria (from the frame doc, §3)

Per participant, record:

| Signal | PASS looks like |
|---|---|
| Current method spans 2+ catalog sites per topic | Yes, described concretely in step 2–3 |
| Hunt is repeated & dissatisfying | Frequency ≥ monthly + a specific pain moment quoted |
| Adopts a book from the fused list their own way missed | Starts reading / saves one in step 10 |

**PASS:** ≥ 4 of 6 on all three signals → proceed to Slice 2 (sort,
pagination, book detail).
**FAIL:** < 3 of 6, or most say their current method "is fine" → the problem
is convenience, not pain: iterate on the framing (e.g. is the real job
"free access" or "quality filtering"?) or kill and celebrate the fast learning.

Triangulate across all interviews before concluding — one enthusiastic
participant is not signal.
